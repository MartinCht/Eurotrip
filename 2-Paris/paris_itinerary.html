⚠️ Tourist Traps to Avoid

  • Avoid restaurants with picture menus or aggressive touts on Champs-Élysées - overpriced and low quality

💡 Practical Tips

  • AVOID Châtelet station if possible - it has ~17 exits and is notoriously confusing to navigate. Use a less chaotic connecting station when there's a choice.
  • Many Paris metro lines split into two branches with different final destinations (e.g., Line 7, Line 13) - ALWAYS read the platform screen/train headsign carefully to confirm which branch you're boarding, not just the line number.
  • RER is faster than the metro for longer distances (fewer stops) but gets very crowded during peak hours (~10:00 and ~18:00) - avoid RER at these times if you can use metro instead.
  • Uber in Paris is relatively inexpensive and the fleet is almost entirely Tesla - a comfortable backup option for late nights or heavy luggage days.
  • Metro is fastest but can be crowded during rush hours (08:00-09:30, 17:30-19:30). Consider biking during these times.
  • Keep metro tickets until you exit - inspectors check and fine €50 for no ticket.
  • Pickpockets active on Line 1 (Louvre-Champs-Élysées) and Line 4 (Gare du Nord-Montparnasse). Keep valuables secure.
  • Many museums closed Mondays or Tuesdays - check before going. Louvre closed Tuesdays, Orsay closed Mondays (this is why Orsay was dropped from this itinerary - Day 3 falls on a Monday).
  • Student ID (ISIC card or university ID) gets discounts at most museums (30-50% off).
  • Free museum entry for EU students under 26 at many national museums (Louvre, Orsay, etc.). Check each museum's policy.
  • Tap water (l'eau du robinet) is free at restaurants - don't pay for bottled water unless you want it.
  • Tipping not required (service included) but rounding up or leaving €1-2 for good service is appreciated.
  • Learn basic phrases: 'Bonjour' (hello), 'Merci' (thank you), 'Pardon' (excuse me), 'L'addition, s'il vous plaît' (the bill, please).
